One record per disease in `batches/olive.jsonl`; each record's `source_url` is its page here.

## Records shipped (6)

1. **Peacock spot — *Venturia oleaginea***
   - UC IPM, "Peacock Spot / Olive / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines" — https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/olive/peacock-spot/
   - Facts taken: black circular spots 2.5–12 mm with yellow halo, upper surface of low-canopy leaves; autumn infection, optimum ~58–75 °F, ~48 h free moisture (as little as 14 h); survival in lesions on leaves remaining on the tree; fall/winter rain-driven infections; summer inactivity; preventive timing late October before winter rains.
   - Name support (also opened/read): Varanda et al. 2019, "Fungal Communities Associated with Peacock and Cercospora Leaf Spots in Olive", Plants/PMC — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6630884/ (Venturia oleaginea = peacock spot; spot description 0.2–1 cm with yellowish halo).

2. **Verticillium wilt — *Verticillium dahliae***
   - UC IPM, "Verticillium Wilt / Olive / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines" — https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/olive/verticillium-wilt/
   - Facts taken: sudden wilt of leaves on one or more branches early in the season; xylem darkening frequently NOT apparent in olive; soil/root survival; microsclerotia survive ≥30 years; defoliating vs non-defoliating strains; avoid soils >1 microsclerotium/g and land with cotton/cucurbits/eggplant/pepper/potato/tomato history; pre-plant flooding and grass cover crops (rye, sudangrass); tolerant Frantoio, Empeltre, Koroneiki (partial), Ascolano, Kalamon; no reliable post-planting control.

3. **Olive knot — *Pseudomonas savastanoi* pv. *savastanoi***
   - UC IPM, "Olive Knot" Pest Notes, UC ANR Publication 74156 — https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/olive-knot/?src=302-www&fr=4341
   - Facts taken: galls at leaf nodes (leaf-scar infection), pruning/frost/hail wounds; growth-regulator-driven proliferation; survival in galls + epiphytic populations; infective bacterial ooze in rain; leaf scars susceptible ≤7 days, pruning wounds ≥14 days; spread by wind-blown rain, contaminated tools, nursery stock; girdling, twig death, off-flavour fruit; dry-season gall removal + tool sterilisation; related pv. *nerii* on oleander.

4. **Anthracnose — *Colletotrichum acutatum* species complex (with *C. gloeosporioides* complex)**
   - Töfoli & Domingues (APTA – Instituto Biológico), "Leaf and fruit diseases in olive cultivation", Revista Cultivar — https://revistacultivar.com/index.php/articles/leaf-and-fruit-diseases-in-olive-cultivation
   - Facts taken (Anthracnose section + Management section): dark depressed circular/irregular fruit lesions with acervuli forming a pinkish-orange gelatinous mass; weight and oil-yield loss, acidic reddish oil; rot/mummification; flower rot, young-fruit drop, branch death; favoured 15–30 °C + high humidity + rain near ripening; latent infections from splash-dispersed conidia off diseased/mummified fruit; sanitation, pruning for aeration, early harvest, label-only fungicide use.

5. **Cercospora leaf spot (cercosporiosis) — *Pseudocercospora cladosporioides***
   - Same Revista Cultivar article (dedicated Cercosporiosis section) — https://revistacultivar.com/index.php/articles/leaf-and-fruit-diseases-in-olive-cultivation
   - Facts taken: mature leaves of lower branches; upper surface irregular yellowish spots turning necrotic brown; lower surface diffuse greyish growth of conidia/conidiophores; depressed greyish-brown fruit lesions, acidic low-quality oil; favoured by high humidity, dense poorly-aerated groves; survival in fallen leaves; germination/penetration 4–24 °C, optimum ~18–22 °C; frequent association with peacock spot.
   - Cross-check (opened): Varanda et al. 2019, PMC — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6630884/ (P. cladosporioides = cercosporiosis; grey areas on the lower leaf surface; the two leaf spots are "frequently difficult to distinguish").

6. **Olive quick decline syndrome — *Xylella fastidiosa* (subsp. *pauca* in Puglia)**
   - EPPO, "Xylella fastidiosa in the EPPO region" (Special Alert short note) — https://www.eppo.int/ACTIVITIES/plant_quarantine/shortnotes_qps/shortnotes_xylella
   - Facts taken: xylem-limited bacterium; first European detection Oct 2013 in Salento (Puglia) olives with extensive leaf scorch and dieback; Puglia subspecies = subsp. *pauca*; xylem blockage → drying, scorching, wilting, death; transmission by xylem-feeding Hemiptera, *Philaenus spumarius* shown to transmit olive-to-olive in Italy; >350 hosts, many symptomless; olive = main economic host in the EPPO region; official eradication/containment measures in IT/FR/ES.
   - EPPO code XYLEFA as given in the EPPO Datasheet for Xylella fastidiosa (gd.eppo.int, read in search results).

## Dropped

- **Sooty mold (Capnodium spp.)** — DROPPED. The UC IPM olive black-scale page (opened: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/olive/black-scale/) documents sooty mold fungus growing on black-scale honeydew on olive, but names no causal genus; the pages that name Capnodium (e.g. Texas Plant Disease Handbook sooty mold entry) are not olive-specific. No single authoritative page both names the organism and treats it on olive was opened, so per SPEC rule zero it gets no record rather than a stitched one.
