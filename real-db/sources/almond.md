# Almond sources — batch 2026-10-30

Every record in `batches/almond.jsonl` cites one actually-opened authoritative page about that exact disease on almond. All 10 cited pages are UC Statewide IPM Program (UC IPM) almond pages, opened and read 2026-10-30. EPPO codes are "" throughout — none of the opened pages showed one, and none was invented. No pesticide product names or dosages are used in the records; chemical control is stated only as following local label / extension guidance.

Naming authority note: several UC IPM pages do not print the pathogen's species name in their extracted text. For those records, the species name comes from the American Phytopathological Society (APS) "Diseases of Almond" list, also opened and read 2026-10-30 (https://www.apsnet.org/edcenter/resources/commonnames/Pages/DiseasesofAlmond.aspx), and the record says so in its evidence rather than attributing the name to UC IPM.

## Records and their sources

1. **Alternaria leaf spot** (`almond_alternaria_leaf_spot`, Alternaria alternata)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Alternaria Leaf Spot / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/alternaria-leaf-spot
   - Used: fairly large brown leaf spots about 12–18 mm, turning black as spores are produced, fastest in June–July, near-complete defoliation by early summer when severe; first and worst on exposed leaves; dew, high humidity, stagnant air favouring; worst in southern San Joaquin Valley and northern Sacramento Valley; most susceptible varieties Carmel, Sonora, Monterey, Winters, Butte; monitoring April–June; DSV forecasting model described.
   - Naming note: UC page says only "Alternaria sp." in the extracted text; Alternaria alternata is from the APS list.

2. **Anthracnose** (`almond_anthracnose`, Colletotrichum acutatum)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Anthracnose / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/anthracnose/
   - Used: blossom blight and fruit infection with spur/limb dieback; infected flowers similar to brown rot strikes; marginal leaf necrosis from water-soaked fading areas, dead leaves remaining attached; round orangish sunken hull lesions about 3 weeks after petal fall, profuse gumming as infection reaches the kernel, mummified fruit remaining on the spur, death of bearing shoots/branches; wood death possibly from a toxin rather than direct colonisation; warm rainy weather favouring; most damaging on Thompson, Merced, Price, Peerless, Winters, Monterey, Fritz, Butte; Nonpareil less susceptible; prune out dead wood; low-angle nozzles for sprinklers; UC timing from 5–10% bloom or pink bud, repeated every 10–14 days if rains persist.
   - Naming note: UC page does not name the species in the extracted text; Colletotrichum acutatum is from the APS list.

3. **Bacterial blast and bacterial canker** (`almond_bacterial_blast_canker`, Pseudomonas syringae pv. syringae)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Bacterial Blast and Bacterial Canker / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/bacterial-canker/
   - Used: two phases of one disease on flowers, leaves, trunks, branches; spring limb dieback with rough cankers and amber gum or total collapse; blast — sudden blossom withering, brown blossom, black peduncle, blossom remaining attached, necrotic shoot tips exuding gum, buds dying, young-leaf necrotic lesions with chlorotic halo; canker — amber gum balls over necrotic bark, flecks coalescing into dead phloem, sour-sap phase with brown fermented sour-smelling inner bark and no gum/canker; survives on plant surfaces, spread by splashing rain, favoured by high moisture and low winter/spring temperatures; almost exclusively where almond or other Prunus orchards previously existed; worse in cold-air low areas and sandy high-ring-nematode areas; nitrogen-deficient trees and trees 2–8 years old most prone; management by preventing predisposing conditions, resistant rootstocks, nutrition, nematode reduction; dormant chemical treatment not successful in California, most strains resistant, phytotoxic to blossoms/young leaves.
   - Naming note: UC page body gives Pseudomonas syringae; pv. syringae is from the APS list. This page was also opened directly by the parent agent and its text verified.

4. **Bacterial spot** (`almond_bacterial_spot`, Xanthomonas arboricola pv. pruni)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Bacterial Spot / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/bacterial-spot
   - Used: fruit infections as small water-soaked circular spots enlarging slightly, becoming necrotic, progressing to mesocarp, endocarp and endosperm, profuse amber gum exudation, multiple lesions causing fruit drop, mummified fruit carrying the pathogen; leaf lesions less common, 0.5–2 mm water-soaked circles mainly along the midrib toward tip/margin, chlorotic then brown and necrotic, some abscising into shot-hole-like holes, coalescing, severe disease defoliating; spread from mummies by dripping dew, splashing water, wind-driven rain, entry through natural openings or wounds, favoured by high moisture and temperatures above 68 °F, severe fruit infection with frequent rainfall/irrigation during fruit development; first reported on almond in California in 2013; Fritz most susceptible, also Nonpareil, Butte, Carmel, Price; air movement, irrigation design, dormant mummy removal, clean harvest equipment; delayed-dormant and in-season protective treatments with reduced rates to avoid phytotoxicity.
   - Naming note: UC IPM current name Xanthomonas arboricola pv. pruni; APS list (2001) gives Xanthomonas campestris pv. pruni as a synonym; the record uses the UC/EPPO current name.

5. **Brown rot blossom blight** (`almond_brown_rot_blossom_blight`, Monilinia laxa)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Brown Rot Blossom Blight / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/brown-rot-blossom-blight
   - Used: collapsed blossom spurs and associated leaves forming shoot blight; gum at the base of infected flowers; twig cankers with tan centres and dark margins; grey-to-tan spore masses on diseased flower parts and twig cankers at high humidity; flowers infectible from pink bud to petal fall, most susceptible when fully open, stigma, anthers and petals all very susceptible; survival in twig cankers and diseased flower parts/spurs, late-winter spore pads; rainy weather with mid-70s °F during bloom favouring, possible in dry high-humidity years; UC timing at pink bud (5–10% bloom) and full bloom, second/third application near full bloom if severe or bloom extended with rainfall; highly susceptible cultivars Drake, Winters, Wood Colony; Butte, Carmel, Ne Plus Ultra very susceptible.
   - Naming note: UC page names neither species in the extracted text; APS lists both Monilinia laxa and Monilinia fructicola for brown rot on almond. The record uses Monilinia laxa and states Monilinia fructicola, and does not present either as the sole UC-given name.

6. **Green fruit rot (jacket rot)** (`almond_green_fruit_rot`, Botrytis cinerea)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Green Fruit Rot (Jacket Rot) / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/green-fruit-rot-jacket-rot
   - Used: begins in the latter part of bloom infecting senescing petals and anthers; water-soaked brown petal spots; infected petals falling onto leaves causing secondary infections; anther infection spreading to floral tubes/jackets, which wither and stick to developing fruit; brown spot where the jacket sticks to the fruit, especially where clusters trap senescing flower parts; frequently rotting entire nut clusters; cool wet bloom conditions favouring; of little importance in many years but severe losses with extended bloom and abundant moisture; UC timing at full bloom when bloom is extended and moisture abundant, continuing until rain stops; treatment must be effective against all potential pathogens.
   - Naming note: multi-pathogen complex. UC extracted text names only Botrytis cinerea; APS also lists Monilinia fructicola, Monilinia laxa and Sclerotinia sclerotiorum. The record uses Botrytis cinerea and states the others, and does not support a single-pathogen field diagnosis.

7. **Hull rot** (`almond_hull_rot`, Monilinia spp.)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Hull Rot / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/hull-rot
   - Used: first indication several weeks before harvest — leaves on a shoot wither and die; fruit on that shoot shows a brown area on the outside of the hull, with tan fungal growth in the brown area inside/outside the hull (Monilinia) or black fungal growth inside the hull (Rhizopus); fungi invade hulls and produce a toxin killing the spur and eventually the attached shoot; shoot death makes nuts harder to remove at harvest and reduces future productivity; hulls susceptible from hull split until dry (10 days to 2 months depending on fertilisation/irrigation); most susceptible important varieties Nonpareil, Monterey, Sonora, Fritz, Winters; hard-shelled Mission, Davey, Drake may show rotted hulls but rarely shoot dieback; most effective control is managing vigour through irrigation and nitrogen; reduce irrigation at hull split, avoid standing water at hull split, avoid excess nitrogen and applications 45–60 days before hull split; timing differs by pathogen (at hull split for Rhizopus, 3–4 weeks before hull split for Monilinia); nuts/leaves stuck on trees after harvest as an indicator.
   - Naming note: multi-pathogen complex — UC page names both Monilinia spp. and Rhizopus stolonifer. The record uses the first-named group, Monilinia spp., and states Rhizopus stolonifer, following the first-named-species precedent used for multi-species records in earlier batches.

8. **Rust** (`almond_rust`, Tranzschelia discolor)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Rust / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/rust
   - Used: small yellow spots on the upper leaf surface, rusty-red appearance on the lower surface when rust-coloured spores erupt, airborne spread to other leaves, young twigs possibly infected but twig lesions seldom seen on almond, severe disease quickly defoliating trees, weakening them, reducing the following year's bloom, often in second- and third-leaf orchards without fungicide; humid conditions (near rivers/streams) favouring, worse with late-spring and summer rain, excessive nitrogen increasing susceptibility, survival in infected leaves and possibly infected twigs; UC timing — protective treatment 5 weeks after petal fall, followed 4–5 weeks later, applied before symptoms appear; hastening autumn leaf fall to prevent winter inoculum build-up.
   - Naming note: UC page says only "the rust fungus" in the extracted text; Tranzschelia discolor is from the APS list.

9. **Scab** (`almond_scab`, Venturia carpophila)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Scab / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/scab/
   - Used: greyish-black soft-looking spots on leaves, fruit and twigs; young lesions first indistinct small yellow specks, best seen holding a leaf up to light; lesions usually not visible until late spring or early summer; severe infections causing early defoliation; trees weakened if uncontrolled for several years; prolonged wet spring weather favouring; most prevalent in the Sacramento Valley; common in sprinkler-irrigated orchards where water reaches foliage; survival in twig lesions, spores spread by wind or rain; dormant-spur sampling and treatment thresholds; avoid sprinkler water on foliage; scab may be controlled by shot-hole spray timing, but a dedicated treatment may be needed if rain continues into mid- to late spring (one application as late as 5 weeks after petal fall can protect, earlier at 2 weeks after petal fall may improve control); documented resistance to one fungicide mode-of-action group.
   - Naming note: UC page says only "the fungus" in the extracted text; Venturia carpophila (teleomorph) / Cladosporium carpophilum (anamorph) are from the APS list. This naming conflict is disclosed in the record.

10. **Shot hole (Coryneum blight)** (`almond_shot_hole`, Wilsonomyces carpophilus)
    - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Shot Hole / Almond / Agriculture: Pest Management Guidelines"
    - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/almond/shot-hole
    - Used: spots on leaves, fruit, twigs, flowers (flower/twig lesions relatively scarce or hard to find); leaf lesions beginning as tiny reddish specks enlarging over several millimetres into spots with tan centres and purplish margins; sporulating fruiting structure as a small dark speck (sporodochium and spores) in the centre of the spot, stated as a diagnostic characteristic; young-leaf spots usually falling out leaving a hole, older leaves retaining lesions; fruit spots small with purplish margins, slightly corky and raised, on the upper fruit surface; heavy young-fruit infection causing fruit drop, distortion and gumming; survival on infected twigs and as spores in healthy buds; spores moved by water; prolonged wetness (rain or sprinkler irrigation) required; yield loss, defoliation, weakened trees; fall/spring monitoring; fall fruiting structures meaning high risk next spring and a petal-fall treatment warranted; spring treatment as soon as fruiting structures are evident, continued at label interval while weather stays conducive; contact protectants only effective with good coverage before a wet period.
    - Naming note: UC IPM spells the genus "Wilsonomyces"; Utah State University's Coryneum blight page (also opened 2026-10-30, https://extension.usu.edu/planthealth/ipm/notes_ag/fruit-coryneum-blight) spells it "Wilsonmyces". The record follows the cited UC IPM spelling. Synonym Stigmina carpophila (APS).
