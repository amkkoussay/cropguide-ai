# Strawberry sources — batch 2026-10-29

Every record in `batches/strawberry.jsonl` cites one actually-opened authoritative page about that exact disease on strawberry. No record rests on model memory or on a search snippet alone. EPPO codes are "" throughout — none of the opened pages showed one, and none was invented.

## Records and their sources

1. **Gray mold** (`strawberry_gray_mold`, Botrytis cinerea)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Botrytis Fruit Rot" (Strawberry Pest Management Guidelines)
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/strawberry/botrytis-fruit-rot/
   - Used: flower infection via spores in free water in cool weather, dormancy in floral tissue resuming on the berry as sugars increase; small brown lesions often under the calyx, gray velvety mold within a day of resumed growth, rapid lesion enlargement, berries keeping shape under a gray-brown spore coat, soft mushy then leathery dry rot; wet cool weather required, damage mostly coastal/northern nurseries; cultural control (remove dead leaves/infected fruit, harvest all ripe plus decayed/rain-damaged fruit, wind-dried sites, plastic mulch, plastic tunnels, necked/reflexed-calyx fruit types). The page's fungicide table (product names and rates) was deliberately NOT repeated in the record.

2. **Powdery mildew** (`strawberry_powdery_mildew`, Podosphaera aphanis)
   - Source: UF/IFAS Extension, "PP-208/PP129: Powdery Mildew of Strawberry"
   - URL: https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/PP129
   - Used: P. aphanis (syn. Sphaerotheca macularis), obligate parasite of living strawberry tissue only; small white patches on lower leaf surface expanding and coalescing, or yellow/reddish-brown spots breaking to the upper surface, upward leaf curl, dark round chasmothecia, flower infection giving aborted/malformed fruit, fuzzy growth on seeds of older fruit; favored by moderate to high humidity and 60–80°F, inhibited by rain/dew/overhead irrigation, worse in greenhouses/plastic tunnels; control via disease-free transplants (primary inoculum source), resistant cultivars, regular early-season surveys; fungicide resistance to multiple classes noted. Product names in the page were NOT repeated in the record.

3. **Anthracnose fruit rot** (`strawberry_anthracnose_fruit_rot`, Colletotrichum acutatum)
   - Source: UF/IFAS Extension, "PP-207/PP130: Anthracnose Fruit Rot of Strawberry"
   - URL: https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/PP130
   - Used: C. acutatum species complex (C. gloeosporioides less frequent); fruit most affected but flowers, crowns, leaves, petioles, roots also susceptible; green-fruit lesions small hard sunken dark brown/black, ripening-fruit lesions larger tan to dark brown, sticky light orange spore ooze in wet weather; flowers highly susceptible, brown and remaining attached; small black spots on button-sized fruit from flower infections; spread first on foliage often symptomlessly, conidia moved by splashing water; favored by warm wet weather, epidemics typically in spring; control by exclusion (pathogen-free nurseries, clean personnel/equipment, avoid old plastic with dead plants), resistant cultivars, advisory-system timing. Product names in the page were NOT repeated in the record.

4. **Verticillium wilt** (`strawberry_verticillium_wilt`, Verticillium dahliae)
   - Source: UMass Amherst CAFE, "Strawberry IPM - Verticillium Wilt"
   - URL: https://www.umass.edu/agriculture-food-environment/fruit/fact-sheets/strawberry-ipm-verticillium-wilt
   - Used: marginal and interveinal browning and collapse of outer leaves, inner leaves stunted but staying green until death, fruiting plants worse, first symptoms as temperatures rise in late spring; microsclerotia viable in soil for many years, penetration of root hairs directly or through wounds, vascular invasion; wide host range especially tomato family; spread by water, wind, infested soil movement, infected planting stock; management (scout after bloom, dig weak-area plants, 5-year rotation to non-hosts avoiding solanaceous crops/squash/raspberries, biofumigant crucifers, disease-free transplants, resistant varieties, well-drained soil, avoid overfertilization, control pigweed/lambsquarters, remove infected plants plus neighbors, do not replant where infected; no chemical control recommended). Note: the page spells the species "Verticillium dahlia" in its heading; the record uses the standard spelling V. dahliae and states both species the page names (V. dahliae and V. albo-atrum) in its evidence framing.

5. **Phytophthora crown rot** (`strawberry_phytophthora_crown_rot`, Phytophthora cactorum)
   - Source: Pacific Northwest Pest Management Handbooks, "Strawberry (Fragaria spp.)-Crown Rot" (rev. March 2026)
   - URL: https://pnwhandbooks.org/plantdisease/host-disease/strawberry-fragaria-spp-crown-rot
   - Used: P. cactorum, oospores in old crown tissue surviving long periods in soil, spring zoospores under saturated soil infecting through wounds, favored by warm wet conditions; young leaves off-colored and suddenly wilting, whole plant wilting collapsing and dying, crown breaking on pulling leaving main crown and roots in the ground, dark brown crown vascular discoloration, roots healthy if this is the only problem, sublethal infections stunting and reducing yield; cultural control (certified plants only, resistant cultivars, well-drained soil without winter standing water where red stele unknown, raised beds 8–10 inches, preplant solarization, avoid draws/swales, clean equipment). The page's chemical table (product names and rates) was deliberately NOT repeated; the record says only that Phytophthora materials do not kill the organism.

6. **Angular leaf spot** (`strawberry_angular_leaf_spot`, Xanthomonas fragariae)
   - Source: UC Statewide IPM Program, "Angular Leaf Spot" (Strawberry Pest Management Guidelines)
   - URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/strawberry/angular-leaf-spot/
   - Used: minute water-soaked spots on lower leaf/calyx surfaces enlarging to translucent angular vein-delimited spots, viscous bacterial ooze drying as a whitish scaly film, coalescing lesions with necrotic reddish-brown upper-surface spots and a chlorotic halo; bacterium not free-living in soil but overwintering on infected plant material, host-specific, highly resistant to degradation, splash-dispersed, favored by cool moist days with cold nights near freezing; uncommon vascular collapse (water-soaked base of newly emerged leaves, then sudden whole-plant death like crown rot); management (certified planting materials, crop rotation, avoid overhead irrigation, drip irrigation; chemical controls typically ineffective). The page's note that copper compounds are registered but phytotoxic was NOT turned into a product recommendation in the record.

7. **Leaf scorch** (`strawberry_leaf_scorch`, Diplocarpon earliana)
   - Source: University of Connecticut IPM Program, "Leaf Diseases of Strawberry"
   - URL: https://ipm.cahnr.uconn.edu/leaf-diseases-of-strawberry/
   - Used (leaf scorch section): D. earliana, middle-aged and older leaves most susceptible, favored by warm wet weather 68–86°F, most spores formed in mid-summer; numerous small irregular purplish spots growing together, centers becoming brownish, whole leaflet purplish to reddish to brown with tiny black dots, leaves drying with edges curling up, scorched look; overwintering effect; shared fungal-leaf-spot prevention (disease-free plants, good air circulation, well-drained soil, narrow rows, drip irrigation preferred, morning overhead irrigation, remove old tissue, mowings removed or plowed under, resistant varieties available for leaf spot and leaf scorch).
   - Note: other sources spell the species Diplocarpon earlianum (Cornell Berry Diagnostic Tool); the record follows its cited UConn page's spelling "earliana" and the difference is disclosed here.

8. **Common leaf spot** (`strawberry_common_leaf_spot`, Mycosphaerella fragariae)
   - Source: University of Connecticut IPM Program, "Leaf Diseases of Strawberry" (same page as above)
   - URL: https://ipm.cahnr.uconn.edu/leaf-diseases-of-strawberry/
   - Used (leaf spot section): M. fragariae infecting only strawberry, middle-aged leaves most susceptible, favored by 68–77°F in warm moist weather; round deep purple spots enlarging to grayish-white centers on older leaves or light brown on younger leaves, distinct reddish-purple to rusty brown border, spots also on fruit (black seed disease), stems, petioles, caps, runners in favorable years; same shared prevention as record 7; resistant varieties available for leaf spot.

9. **Red stele** (`strawberry_red_stele`, Phytophthora fragariae var. fragariae)
   - Source: Pacific Northwest Pest Management Handbooks, "Strawberry (Fragaria spp.)-Red Stele" (rev. March 2026)
   - URL: https://pnwhandbooks.org/plantdisease/host-disease/strawberry-fragaria-spp-red-stele
   - Used: P. fragariae var. fragariae, soil-infesting, living many years in fields, active in cool wet weather, attacking roots soon after fall rains and remaining active through winter, spreading fastest with poor drainage or heavier soils, low areas typically infected; root core reddish pink turning cinnamon-brown with outer cortex staying white, ultimately black, lateral roots destroyed giving rat-tail main roots, undernourished plants, severe plants stunted with bluish-green glossy-lacking young leaves and older leaves red/orange/yellow, eventually wilting and dying; several races (resistant in one field may be susceptible in another); cultural control (certified plants only, well-drained soil without winter standing water where red stele unknown, raised beds 8–10 inches or drain furrows, preplant solarization, avoid draws/swales, deep chiseling, clean equipment, resistant cultivars Olympus/Hood/Totem/Rainier/Shuksan with the race caveat). The page's chemical table was NOT repeated; the record says only that curative treatments are not recommended and materials do not kill the organism.

10. **Phomopsis leaf blight** (`strawberry_phomopsis_leaf_blight`, Phomopsis obscurans)
    - Source: University of Connecticut IPM Program, "Leaf Diseases of Strawberry" (same page as records 7–8)
    - URL: https://ipm.cahnr.uconn.edu/leaf-diseases-of-strawberry/
    - Used (leaf blight section): P. obscurans affecting only strawberry, worldwide, sporadic in New England; one to six round reddish-purple spots on the leaflet, usually older leaflets late in the season, spots following major veins to become V-shaped, light brown inside with dark brown edges, purple/red/yellow border possible, tiny black dots in spot centers, whole leaflet possibly browning; stolons, petioles, fruit stems infectable and killable if girdled, caps and fruit also infected; shared life cycle and warm moist favoring with the other two fungal leaf spots; no resistant varieties available for leaf blight (unlike leaf spot and leaf scorch); same shared prevention as record 7.
