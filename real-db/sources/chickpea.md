# Chickpea sources — batch 2026-10-28

Every record in `batches/chickpea.jsonl` cites one actually-opened authoritative page about that exact disease on chickpea. All 10 records cite the North Dakota State University Extension diagnostic guide, cited by section — the same section-citation pattern used for the ICARDA guide (date palm, 2026-10-24) and the CSU soil-borne page (onion, 2026-10-27). No record rests on model memory or on a search snippet alone. EPPO codes are "" throughout — none of the opened pages showed one, and none was invented.

## Records and their sources

1. **Ascochyta blight** (`chickpea_ascochyta_blight`, Ascochyta rabiei)
   - Section used: "Ascochyta blight". Used: most serious chickpea disease worldwide; all aerial parts; water-soaked irregular/circular-oval leaf lesions with concentric rings of small brown pycnidia; elongate stem lesions at nodes, girdling, breakage; shriveled brown seed; residue survival, seed-borne, host range limited to chickpeas; favored 60–77 °F with frequent rainfall; minimum three-year rotation, less susceptible cultivars, disease-free seed.

2. **Fusarium wilt** (`chickpea_fusarium_wilt`, Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceri)
   - Section used: "Fusarium wilt". Used: symptoms typically at flowering; drooping wilted dull-green leaves, sometimes one-sided; yellowing/wilting/death lower canopy upward; dark brown to black vascular tissue near stem base; warm soil 77–86 °F; short rotations (two years or less); infected seed/susceptible cultivars; two pathotypes (slow yellowing vs wilt); US races 0, 1A, 1B/C, 5, 6; confused with Fusarium root rot and waterlogging.
   - Note: the record keeps the guide's spelling "ciceri" (not "ciceris") because that is what the cited page prints. A parallel researcher reports that Plant Health Australia's chickpea Fusarium fact sheet uses "ciceris" — a spelling variant across sources, not an error in this record; the Oct 30 merge should not "correct" one to the other without noting the variant.

3. **Botrytis gray mold** (`chickpea_botrytis_gray_mold`, Botrytis cinerea)
   - Section used: "Botrytis gray mold". Used: water-soaked lesions on leaves, stems, flowers, pods turning gray to dark brown; fluffy gray mold in humidity; flower drop, seed abortion; brown stem speckling, girdling lesions; pods brown then gray from sporulation; favored by infested seed, dense canopy, humidity above 95%, 68–77 °F; host range over 100 species; seed-borne, soil and residue survival; rapid progress; foliar treatments poorly effective inside dense canopy.

4. **Dry root rot** (`chickpea_dry_root_rot`, Macrophomina phaseolina)
   - Section used: "Dry Root Rot". Used: rapidly dying scattered plants in reproductive stages; dry dark taproot without laterals, lower taproot often missing, plants pull up easily; straw-colored plants, brittle rotten primary taproot; black microsclerotia on stem exterior and interior; favored by hot dry conditions (daily minimum above 68 °F, maximum above 86 °F), drought stress, poor/sandy soil; seed-borne and soil-borne, microsclerotia survive over 12 months; host range over 500 species; resistant cultivars available; confused with Fusarium wilt and other root rots.

5. **Rhizoctonia wet root rot** (`chickpea_rhizoctonia_wet_root_rot`, Rhizoctonia solani)
   - Section used: "Rhizoctonia seed, seedling, and wet root rot". Used: seed decay and damping-off, poor emergence; sunken reddish to brown lesions on epicotyl and taproot; older lesions black as secondary microbes invade; patchy to widespread distribution; favored by cool wet soils and herbicide carryover damage; residues and soil harbor the pathogen; wide host range including faba bean, dry bean, field pea, lentil, soybean, canola, sunflower; commonly in a complex with other root rots.

6. **Sclerotinia stem and crown rot** (`chickpea_sclerotinia_stem_rot`, Sclerotinia sclerotiorum)
   - Section used: "Sclerotinia stem and crown rot". Used: girdling stem lesions causing senescence and wilt; lesions initially light brown, bleached and brittle with age; fluffy white growth at lesion edges; black hardened structures in/on mature lesions; favored by wet weather with daytime highs in the 70 to low 80 °F range, dense canopy, tight broadleaf rotations (especially lentils, sunflowers, canola); sclerotia persist many years; often concurrent with Ascochyta blight and Botrytis gray mold; preventive action needed for control.
   - Note: the section names S. sclerotiorum first and also lists S. minor and S. trifoliorum; the record uses the first-named species and states the others in its evidence.

7. **Alternaria blight** (`chickpea_alternaria_blight`, Alternaria alternata)
   - Section used: "Alternaria Blight". Used: all above-ground parts; small water-soaked circular lesions turning reddish brown to purple; infected flowers/leaves straw-colored before falling; shriveled blackened seed; moist conditions make plants appear black from sporulation; favored by infected seed, susceptible cultivars, 75–82 °F, humidity above 85%, older plants more susceptible; seed survival up to 20 months; broad host range (lentil, pea, mungbean, cowpea); infected seed may be unfit for food/feed; confused with Ascochyta or Stemphylium blight.

8. **Stemphylium blight** (`chickpea_stemphylium_blight`, Stemphylium spp.)
   - Section used: "Stemphylium blight". Used: large irregular field patches; initial lesions small, roughly circular, brown, merging into irregular shapes covering large areas; older leaf lesions with yellow or gray borders; leaf loss; small elongated brown stem spots; favored by cool 59–68 °F and high humidity, excessive vegetative growth, at and after flowering; transmitted on residue, soil and seed; minor importance in the US; no fungicides registered for control; confused with Alternaria and Ascochyta blight.
   - Note: the guide names the pathogen only as "Stemphylium species", so the record uses Stemphylium spp. and says so in its evidence; no species is invented.

9. **Verticillium wilt** (`chickpea_verticillium_wilt`, Verticillium dahliae)
   - Section used: "Verticillium wilt". Used: leaves yellow, wilt and die; light brown vascular discoloration in stem; uneven leaflet necrosis moving up the stem; favored by warm moist soils 61–68 °F and warm air 72–82 °F, susceptible cultivars; spread in irrigation water, on machinery, via infested seed; soil survival up to 10 years; broad host range (woody and herbaceous); rotation to non-host small grains, equipment sanitation; confused with Fusarium wilt and drought stress.
   - Note: the section names both V. dahliae and V. albo-atrum; the record uses the first-named species and states the other in its evidence.

10. **Pea enation mosaic** (`chickpea_pea_enation_mosaic`, Pea enation mosaic virus)
    - Section used: "Pea enation mosaic". Used: small yellow spots/flecks on leaves; severe stunting; malformed leaves and pods; "windows" of yellow spots and streaks on distorted curled leaves; favored by virus-carrying aphids such as the pea aphid, warm spring temperatures favoring early aphid development and migration; early infections more severe; all current commercial cultivars susceptible; not seed-transmitted; pea, lentil, faba bean and vetch also susceptible; confused with thrips, herbicide or other virus damage.

## Honest notes / drops

- Chickpea rust (Uromyces ciceris-arietini) was NOT included: a parallel researcher searched for a dedicated EPPO/CABI/extension/national-institute page about rust specifically on chickpea and found only slide decks, an encyclopedia entry and a 1987 first-report abstract — no dedicated accepted source page. No rust facts are asserted anywhere in this batch.
- Chickpea collar rot (Sclerotium rolfsii) and chickpea chlorotic stunt virus were verified by the parallel researcher from other accepted sources (GRDC chickpea guide section and a Plant Health Australia contingency plan), but the batch already reached 10 records from the single NDSU guide, so they were not needed today and are left as spare candidates for any future top-up — not counted, not written.
- No pesticide product names or dosages appear in any record; where the guide mentions seed or foliar treatments, records say only "follow local extension guidance".
